The JavaGuide Knowledge Planet is a long-running **community for backend and AI application development and career growth**. It is mainly for people preparing for campus or experienced-hire interviews, changing careers, or improving their technical skills. It is also useful for backend developers who want to expand into AI application development.

Backend development remains the community's foundation. We continue to update content on Java fundamentals, databases, Spring, Redis, message queues, distributed systems, high availability, system design, and interview preparation. AI application development is now a major focus, with content on LLM APIs, prompt and context engineering, RAG, agents, MCP, evaluation, and AI system design. Hands-on projects show how to integrate LLM capabilities into backend services.

The AI content focuses on application development and engineering practice, not model training, algorithm research, or paper reproduction. Java backend developers can still use their experience with databases, caches, queues, authentication, asynchronous jobs, and reliability in AI applications.

If you need structured backend interview materials, want help improving your resume, need a backend or AI project for your portfolio, or want advice tailored to your situation, this article explains what the JavaGuide Knowledge Planet offers, who it is for, and why you might join.

After about a year of consideration, I decided on **December 29, 2019** to start my own community and help people learn Java and prepare for Java interviews. Six years have passed. Thank you for staying with me. I'll continue to maintain this community, keep backend development at its core, and add AI application development content without letting down the readers who trust me.

![Date the community was founded](https://oss.javaguide.cn/xingqiu/640-20230727145252757.png)

I was among the earlier technology creators to launch a Knowledge Planet, and one of the few who kept maintaining it. (Many creators stop maintaining their communities after an initial push for sign-ups.) For the first year or two, I did it purely out of enthusiasm. The original price was very low—about the cost of a meal—and I was busy with my new job, so the service was more limited than it is today.

The price and revenue gradually increased. But since many members are students, the price is still much lower than that of similar communities. I also have no plans to run a boot camp, even though it could earn more given the size of my audience.

**I have my principles: no exploiting members, useful content, and a sincere effort to help people.**

## Is It Paid?

Yes, membership is paid. **Why charge a fee?**

1. Maintaining the community takes time and effort. During interview season, I often review resumes and answer questions late into the night. A single resume review elsewhere can cost more than 200 yuan, and resume reviews are only a small part of what the community offers. I also write exclusive interview columns and project guides, each of which is worth more than the membership fee.
2. The community offers many services. I couldn't keep up if I provided them all for free. A fee helps me focus on people who genuinely want this support.
3. Free things can come at a cost. Once you join, there are no additional paid services; everything else is included.
4. A reasonable fee rewards the work I put in and motivates me to keep creating. The income also helps support my family. It's tiring, but worthwhile.
