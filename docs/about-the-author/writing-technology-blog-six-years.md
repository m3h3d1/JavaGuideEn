I've been writing a technology blog for six years. It feels like a small milestone.

At first, I simply wrote up what I learned in class, such as networking and operating systems. Gradually, I started publishing more systematic explanations of technical topics and common interview questions.

![JavaGuide home page](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/image-20230408131717766.png)

Many people want to write a technical blog but aren't sure what they would get out of it. Some start writing, then don't know how to keep going or what to write about. I'll share my thoughts and experience here in the hope that they help answer those questions.

## Why Write a Technical Blog?

### Learn More Effectively and Deepen Your Understanding

Most people have heard of the **Feynman technique**, a learning method whose effectiveness has been demonstrated in practice. It is named after physicist Richard Feynman, who received the Nobel Prize in Physics and participated in the Manhattan Project.

The basic idea is that after learning something new, imagine yourself as a teacher. Explain the complex ideas in simple, direct language, ideally without jargon, so that someone outside the field can understand. Imagine explaining it to an eight-year-old or someone over eighty—even they should be able to follow.

![Teaching others is one of the best ways to learn](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/v2-19373c2e61873c5083ee4b1d1523f8f5_720w.png)

Reading books and watching videos are passive forms of learning and often less effective. The Feynman technique is active learning, which works much better.

**Writing a technical blog is a way of teaching others.** You can use technical terms, unless your audience is nontechnical, but explain ideas in your own words so readers can understand them. **Don't copy from books or paste someone else's summary!**

When we learn something passively, we may only learn enough to use it. We don't dig into how it works, and may not understand key concepts.

Summarizing what you've learned in a blog post makes you think more deeply about it. To explain a topic clearly, you may need to consult many sources or read the source code. Those small efforts deepen your understanding over time.

I've often had this experience: **while writing a post, I suddenly realize that my understanding of a topic was wrong.**

**Writing is a process of reviewing and thinking through what you've learned. It also records your learning journey. As time passes and you grow older, those records become a valuable part of your personal history.**

### Help Others and Find a Sense of Achievement

Just as programmers hope people will appreciate and enjoy their products, we also want our technical writing to resonate with readers.

**When your writing helps someone, it gives you a sense of achievement and happiness.**

![Appreciation from readers](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/image-20230404181906257.png)

That sense of achievement and happiness is **positive feedback** that can motivate you to keep writing.

Even if many readers praise your work, stay humble and keep learning. There are always people who know more than you. Be open to learning from them.

You may also face criticism. People might say your writing lacks depth, that you have too much free time, or that your topic is already covered online or in books.

**Take criticism in stride, focus on your own path, and let your work speak for itself.**

### You May Earn Some Extra Income

Blogging may also bring in some income. Being paid for work that provides value is a good outcome.

Why “may”? **Most people find it difficult to earn money from blogging in the short term. I don't recommend starting a blog just to make money; that can make your writing too transactional and less effective. If you've written for six months and earned nothing, you may lose the motivation to continue.**

I started blogging in my second year of college and began publishing on my WeChat public account during my third year. It wasn't until the second semester of my final year that I earned my first money from writing.

I made it through a promotion for a training company on my WeChat account. If I remember correctly, it paid around **500 yuan**. That wasn't much, but as a college student, it meant a lot to me. I realized then that writing could earn money, which made me more eager to share. Unfortunately, the company went out of business after I accepted two promotions from it.

I didn't get another advertising offer for a long time. Then NetEase approached me about a course partnership. It paid 1,000 yuan per article, and I wrote nearly one article a month for almost two years. That became a fairly steady source of income during college.

![Course partnership with NetEase](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/image-20230408115720135.png)

Many longtime readers first found me through JavaGuide. I created the project in my third year while preparing for fall recruitment interviews. To my surprise, it became popular and at one point topped GitHub's rankings. There may not have been many open-source documentation and tutorial projects like it in China at the time, which helped it find an audience.

![JavaGuide star history](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/image-20230408131849198.png)

After the project became popular, a major Chinese cloud provider contacted me and offered to sponsor it. I was excited but cautious, worried it might be a scam. After reviewing the contract several times, I agreed to place the company's banner on the project home page for 1,000 yuan per month.

Over time, as I wrote more popular articles for a broader audience, my blog became better known and my writing income increased.

### Build Your Professional Reputation

Technical writing lets you demonstrate your skills and experience to a wider audience. If you keep sharing quality articles, you can build a reputation in your field.

A professional reputation can help with your job search, paid knowledge-sharing, or publishing a book.

Editors from several well-known publishers have approached me about writing a book. That's an opportunity many people dream of, but I turned them all down because I don't think I'm ready to write a book.

![An editor from the Publishing House of Electronics Industry invited me to write a book](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/image-20230408121132211.png)

The main reason I haven't written one is that it sounds like a lot of work. The process has many steps, and I'm a fairly relaxed person who doesn't want to give all my time to work.

## How Can You Keep Writing?

**People are naturally inclined to be lazy. We need a goal or motivation to push ourselves.**

For technical writing, you could set a target for the number of articles you publish:

- Decide how many articles to write in a year. I find a year too long to set a useful target.
- Publish one high-quality technical article each month. That's more manageable, and it adds up to 12 articles a year.

But focusing only on article count can become too transactional. Quality matters just as much. A high-quality post may take a week or even half a month of your spare time. Don't chase quantity at the expense of quality or lose sight of why you started writing.

My own goal is: **each month, write at least one original technical article or carefully revise and improve three older articles.** (Recommendations of open-source projects, guides to learning projects, personal stories, and interview experiences don't count.)

This goal is manageable for me, so I don't end up rushing just to meet it. When I'm in a good place and work is not too busy, I often exceed it. If counting articles feels too transactional, you can write at your own pace. But if you don't set any intention, you may forget about it and gradually lose interest.

After writing an article, publish it on your own blog and share it on popular Chinese technology communities such as CNBlogs and Juejin. **Sharing it elsewhere can help you reach more readers, get positive feedback that motivates you, and receive suggestions. This is an important part of sustaining your writing habit—don't overlook it!**

Finishing an article that you're proud of can bring real happiness and a sense of achievement. **But getting yourself to write it can be hard.** It's easy to find excuses to go out for fun; sitting down to study or write often takes an extra push.

## What Should You Write About?

These are some useful topics for a technical blog:

1. **Explain a technical concept in depth.** Include your own reasoning instead of piecing together other people's work. Cover the basic concepts and principles, and use practical examples and scenarios.
2. **Describe troubleshooting or performance optimization.** Explain the context and solution in detail: what happened, the root cause, your reasoning, and the steps you took. Include enough practical detail to help readers understand and apply it.
3. **Record your source-code exploration.** Start with a feature, explain how the underlying code implements it, and share what you learned.

Most importantly, follow Markdown conventions. Otherwise, even good content can look unprofessional.

See the [Markdown Style Guide](../javaguide/contribution-guideline.md). It's important; following it will also help when you write documentation at work.

## Any Writing Tips?

### Keep Sentences Short

Avoid long sentences. Short ones (but not fragments) are easier to read and understand.

### Make Your Writing Engaging

Use vivid examples, humor, idioms, or familiar expressions where appropriate. The right style depends on your audience and topic.

### Use Clear, Simple Language

Avoid jargon and complex wording that readers may not understand.

Focus on clarity and persuasion. Simple writing can be persuasive: a strong argument in five sentences can move people more than a hundred dazzling ones. The concise, direct style of maxims and proverbs is part of what makes them compelling.

### Use Visuals

Charts and images can make plain text easier to understand. Add them where they help communicate your ideas.

### Know Your Audience

Before writing, consider who the article is for. Then adjust your style and level of detail to their needs and background.

### Review and Revise

Review and revise your article before publishing. This helps you catch errors, clarify confusing information, and improve the overall quality.

**Good writing comes from revision. Remember that!**

## In Summary

Technical writing can benefit both you and your readers. You may learn a lot from it, and your writing may help others. But it takes time, so balance it with work and the rest of your life.
