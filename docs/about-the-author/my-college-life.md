> For my middle- and high-school years, see [I Was Once an Internet-Addicted Teenager](./internet-addiction-teenager.md), which I wrote in 2020.

I graduated in June 2019, three years before writing this. With the college entrance exams and graduation season here, I want to share a little about my university life.

Here is my story.

I graduated from Yangtze University's Jingzhou campus, an ordinary university outside China's Project 985 and Project 211 programs.

I enjoyed my four years there and still miss them.

Those years were varied. Sometimes I stayed up all night coding; other times I went out at midnight with classmates to walk the old city wall or play League of Legends at an internet cafe until morning.

I wrote this to remember those years and to remind myself to keep moving forward without losing sight of where I started.

## First Year

In my first year, I spent most of my time on extracurricular activities rather than programming.

Perhaps everything in a new city interested me, or perhaps I was still unsure what direction to take.

My new classmates and I explored Jingzhou, visiting the museum, Yangtze River Bridge, Zhang Juzheng's former residence, and Guandi Temple.

![A class outing in my first year](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/41239dd7d18642f7af201292ead94f1a~tplv-k3u1fbpfcp-zoom-1.image.png)

I was hopeful about the future and imagined what working life would be like.

I remember a conversation among the six of us sharing a room. My five roommates thought a future salary of RMB 6,000 a month would be good. I said, “Surely it should be at least 8,000!” They fell silent and thought I was naive.

In truth, I hoped to start at RMB 10,000 a month but was embarrassed to say so.

I am somewhat introverted and dislike being the center of attention. At the start of university, I lacked confidence and hesitated to try things. I wanted to change.

With plenty of enthusiasm, I tried things I had never done: **camping**, **outdoor barbecues**, **speaking on a bus**, **running around the old city wall**, **hiking**, **a survival challenge away from home**, **selling apples at Christmas**, and **performing at a New Year's party**.

My club friends and I prepared the items below in our spare time and sold almost all of them during Christmas week. We even visited every dorm room to sell more.

### Running Tutoring Classes

During the summer after my first year, I organized five tutoring classes in small towns around Xiaogan. We had planned seven but canceled two.

We started almost from scratch, renting rooms, tables, and chairs and recruiting students.

Every weekend I traveled from Jingzhou to Xiaogan and between its counties. Most of the time I went alone, though club friends occasionally joined me.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/6ee6358c236144d8a8a205cc6bc99b9b~tplv-k3u1fbpfcp-zoom-1.image.png)

I remember that Xiaogan had serious flooding that year.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/javaguide/image-20210820201908759.png)

Once I nearly failed to return to school for my final exams. I had not prepared, but I passed every course and did well in several. My grades still suffered enough that I later missed out on scholarship opportunities.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/3c5fe7af43ba4e348244df1692500fce~tplv-k3u1fbpfcp-zoom-1.image.png)

I was short on time and took the train back. Someone on the train even had the same suitcase as mine.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/570f5791aeb54fa1a76892b69e46fec2~tplv-k3u1fbpfcp-zoom-1.image.png)

On those trips, the cheapest hotel I stayed in cost RMB 15. Yes, 15. It was a run-down room in an old house, with no private bathroom, dirty bedding, and nowhere to shower.

The place below was the most luxurious one I stayed in. Heavy rain started after my bus reached Xiaogan, so I found a relatively inexpensive room near the station.

I often bargained intensely with landlords to rent rooms for less.

Honestly, I disliked doing that. I cared too much about saving face and lacked confidence.

Traveling between towns meant spending all day in the sun. By mealtime, even a simple plate of vegetables made me eat several bowls of rice.

I was a picky eater, but I learned that almost anything tastes good when you are hungry.

For more than a month, I cooked alone for six teachers, about ten students, and the landlords' family. My cooking improved a great deal.

The students ranged from primary to middle school and were generally well behaved. Many lived with grandparents while their parents worked away from home.

There were four teachers including me. I mainly taught middle- and high-school physics.

The students got along with us. Two mischievous primary-school children held a grudge after I scolded them and wrote something that made me laugh. They were adorable.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/3680cead2c0f4165bb4865f038326b61~tplv-k3u1fbpfcp-zoom-1.image.png)

I wonder how those students are doing now. I miss them.

I faced a difficult choice: close friends in the club wanted me to help lead it so it could continue.

I had already planned to devote my second year to learning programming and building technical skills.

In the end, I agreed to lead the club with my friends. Genuine friends are hard to find.

## Third Year

Throughout my third year, I had almost no weekends or leisure time. I studied alone in the dorm most of the time and occasionally went to the library or an office.

My roommates often played games or watched shows, but it did not bother me. Once I put on headphones, I felt I had a world of my own.

Unlike many others, I studied more effectively in the dorm than in the library or office.

### Entering a Software Design Competition

I have a regret from that year. Some friends and I entered a software design competition, and the system we spent nearly two months building reached the next round.

I later withdrew because I thought spending more time on it would teach me little and waste time. The team fell apart.

Looking back, I could have learned more. I was overconfident and too eager to move on to bigger things.

I still feel sorry for the friends who had worked late with me.

Life is long, and looking back will always reveal things you regret.

### Deciding Against Graduate School

I considered graduate school because my university was not prestigious and another degree could improve my credentials.

I ultimately decided against it. I was confident I could find a good job without a graduate degree.

### Internship

In my third year, I interned at a nearby company founded by an alumnus. The experience was poor: I learned little and lost time I had planned to spend on other things.

Many projects used older technology such as JSP. That was understandable for legacy projects, but even a project started only a few months earlier used it.

They expected me to start working immediately, assigned more than I could finish, and wanted unpaid overtime.

I had few alternatives: I could not find another internship in Jingzhou or easily move to another city. That was one disadvantage of attending university outside a larger city.

## Fourth Year

### Starting the Job Search

I understood the importance of a university's location when I began looking for internships and jobs.

Few strong employers recruited at my ordinary university in Jingzhou.

Suning was one of the few promising options, but its recruiter began pushing down salary expectations and asking about overtime in the first interview. I lost interest.

I made a serious mistake during autumn recruiting: I applied too late. I waited until my university project was nearly finished; by then summer was almost over and the recruiting season was nearing its end.

The university environment may have contributed. None of my classmates were participating in autumn recruiting; they spent the third-year summer in an internship organized by the department. I stayed at school to work on a project instead.

Universities should remind students to take advantage of autumn recruiting.

After applying online, I received no response from many companies, even when I thought I had done reasonably well on their written tests.

I grew anxious and traveled from Jingzhou to Wuhan to attend recruiting events at universities such as Wuhan University and Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

I spent a day finding an apartment through Danke, then attended a recruiting event at Wuhan University of Technology the next day.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/college-life/image-20210820204919942.png)

That day, I interviewed with my first company, **Xuanwu Technology**.

Even at this small-to-medium company, many candidates came from Wuhan University and Huazhong University of Science and Technology. Like me, many may simply have been looking for interview practice and confidence.

The process took about three days, and I received an offer, though I did not accept it.

### Receiving an Offer

Before coming to Wuhan, I had applied to **ThoughtWorks** online and passed its assignment.

ThoughtWorks was my first choice. I told myself that if I got its offer, I would stop interviewing.

Its process was slow, however, so I came to Wuhan to interview elsewhere as a backup.

I eventually received the ThoughtWorks offer I wanted.

## A Few Suggestions

Here are some suggestions. I am not exceptional, but perhaps you can do better:

1. Decide whether you want graduate school or a job. For graduate school, study the relevant subjects, keep coding, and build a project if possible; it can help with the entrance interview and your skills. For a job, choose a direction early, make a plan, and understand your strengths and weaknesses.
2. Start studying with your job goals in mind early. A clear focus can reduce uncertainty and detours.
3. Learn to study independently and make it a habit.
4. Do not assume skipping class makes someone a bad student. I skipped many classes, mostly ones that I considered less important and that would not prevent graduation, and used the time to study what I valued more.
5. University relationships can be sincere. If you meet someone you like, get to know them; if they do not feel the same, respect that. Attraction can begin with appearance before you get to know someone's personality.
6. Take care of your fitness and go for a run occasionally.
7. Do not focus exclusively on GPA. I found it less relevant to jobs and graduate entrance exams, though passing courses matters. GPA does carry substantial weight for scholarships and admission without an entrance exam.
8. Do not expect an immediate payoff from everything you learn or do. Persistence is difficult when every action must produce an immediate benefit.

## Afterword

Job seekers, especially those from ordinary universities, may encounter education filters. I do not consider that inherently unfair; I hold myself responsible for not entering a more prestigious university.

**Given recruiting costs and time, companies often prefer to select candidates from better-known universities.**

I once regretted not attending a Project 211 or 985 university. On reflection, I felt responsible for that outcome, and computer science seemed somewhat more open than some other fields to people from less prestigious schools.

I know many people from vocational colleges and less prestigious universities who joined major companies. I mention them to encourage readers with similar backgrounds.

**Act more and complain less.**
