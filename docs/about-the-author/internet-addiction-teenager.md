> I wrote this article shortly before China's 2021 college entrance exams.

The college entrance exam brings back a lot of memories for people. Using it as an excuse, I'd like to share a little about my high school years.

My school years were far from ordinary—almost surreal at times—so I have a lot to say. I'll cover middle school and high school, but keep each part brief.

**Everything below is true. I haven't exaggerated anything, even if some of it sounds a little unreal.**

## My First Experience with Computers

I first used a computer when I was in fifth grade. We didn't have one at home, so I started going online at unlicensed internet cafés.

An unlicensed café looked something like this: a crowded, windowless room full of old computers.

![An unlicensed internet café](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/internet-addiction-teenager/heiwangba.png)

Going to these cafés was not always easy. The police sometimes came to check for minors—almost everyone there was underage—and older kids often harassed me. They would take my computer and threaten me for my money. I was alone and timid, and after being beaten up a few times, I tried to avoid the hours when those kids were there and carried as little cash as possible. I was independent from a young age and usually didn't tell my family about problems outside the home; I didn't feel I could rely on them for much.

I can't remember whether my brother or sister first took me to an internet café. I think it was my sister.

At first, I played popular games such as _Meteor Butterfly Sword_ and a motorcycle racing game. I wasn't addicted yet; I just thought they were fun. I could play all afternoon and still hate to leave.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/github/javaguide/books2a6021b9-e7a0-41c4-b69e-a652f7bc3e12-20200802173601289.png)

## Gaming Became an Addiction After Primary School

I started getting hooked after I began playing **QQ Speed**, near the end of sixth grade. That game had me completely captivated. **In class, I would imagine myself drifting around the track. I thought I was the Drift King of Mount Akina!**

I was pretty good. As far as I could tell, no one else at the café could beat me—we often challenged each other.

QQ Speed was popular at the time, so many people born in the 1990s may remember it.

Back then, you didn't need an ID to go online. A prepaid card cost 10 yuan, and an hour online cost one yuan. I often skipped breakfast to save money for the café. Whenever I had cash, I'd run there with my friends to play QQ Speed. What a throwback!

> Writing this made me open my Windows PC, install WeGame, and download QQ Speed.

I stopped playing around eighth grade, and my level stayed at **120**. Leveling was extremely slow then, so that was quite high.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/javaguide/b488618c-3c25-4bc9-afd4-7324e27553bd-20200802175534614.png)

## My Gaming Addiction Peaked in Eighth Grade

My addiction peaked in eighth grade. I was obsessed with **CrossFire**, even more than QQ Speed. In class, I would picture myself picking up a gun and wiping out the other team. I couldn't focus on school at all.

I often got up early to use a computer someone had booked overnight; I didn't have much money for school. Almost every Friday night, I snuck out after my family fell asleep to play all night. I did this countless times in eighth grade, and that's how I ruined my eyesight.

Gaming addiction can make you do anything just to get online. My family lived on the top floor, below a rooftop insulation area. To sneak out without being noticed, I climbed out of my bedroom window, crossed several buildings and empty rooftop spaces, then went downstairs. Looking back, it was dangerous. I'm naturally afraid of the dark, but I was never scared while sneaking out to play. I can't believe how fearless I was back then.

![Snow seen from my rooftop](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/image-20230429114622340.png)

After playing all night on Friday, I'd sleep until noon and then go back to the café. On Sunday, I'd play from around 8 a.m. until 9 or 10 p.m. I had so much energy and never felt tired; I loved every minute.

My final rank in CrossFire was two diamonds. Anyone who played back then knows how many matches that took; leveling up is easier now.

P.S. I returned to CrossFire almost a year ago. I'm now a three-star Lieutenant Colonel.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/cf.png)

My grades were poor. At an ordinary county-level high school with about 500 students in the grade, I usually ranked around 280th. I didn't study physics at all in eighth grade: I slept through class and handed in blank exams.

Why did I hate physics? Early in the school year, the teacher mistakenly thought I was eating in class and lying about it, then slapped me. I held a grudge until college and used to think I'd beat him up someday.

## I Started Studying Seriously in Ninth Grade

In the first semester of ninth grade, I suddenly realized I needed to get serious about school if I wanted to attend a high school near home.

Maybe it wasn't quite an awakening. I mainly wanted to go to the local No. 2 High School because it was near home and surrounded by internet cafés. I could also commute from home. At my middle school, you generally needed to rank in the top 80 to have a chance of getting in. In my first monthly exam of ninth grade, I jumped from around 280th to above 50th. I was now in the running. Because I had improved so much, they named me a **Most Improved Student** and asked me to give a speech to the whole grade. It was my first time speaking to such a large audience. I was nervous, but it felt great—and I impressed the person I had a crush on.

I was still addicted to games in ninth grade, but I only played after finishing all my assignments and paid more attention in class.

I stayed up gaming less often, but still snuck out after my family was asleep and returned around 2 a.m.

Local high schools allowed top students from each middle school to take an **early admission exam**. If you passed, you could skip the regular high school entrance exam. I was lucky enough to take it and got into the local No. 2 High School.

The night before the exam, I waited until midnight, when my mother was asleep, then went to an internet café to play CrossFire. I got home after 3 a.m. and was caught. My mother was waiting in the living room. After she scolded me, I promised not to sneak out at night again.

> One thing to clarify: I know I'm of fairly average intelligence. My improvement was mainly because I had a decent foundation, especially in English and physics. I liked English and had already learned much of the middle-school curriculum in primary school. Physics is stranger: I barely paid attention to it in eighth grade and couldn't solve the problems, but something clicked in ninth grade. I still don't know why. English and physics remained my best subjects in high school. In college, I tutored high school students in physics as a part-time job.

## From the Top Class to a Regular Class

![Back at my high school after receiving my exam results](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/internet-addiction-teenager/wodegaozhong.png)

Because I passed the early admission exam, I started high school four months early in an advanced class.

In class, I secretly read novels such as _Throne of Seal_, _Soul Land_, and _Battle Through the Heavens_. At home around lunchtime and in the evening, I played a few rounds of _Dungeon Fighter Online_ (DNF). My family had bought a computer as a reward for getting me into the No. 2 High School. By the time I uninstalled DNF, I had four max-level characters and two more close to the cap.

I even had a dedicated album on my social profile filled with DNF screenshots. I was obsessed with leveling up and farming dungeons.

Less than a month after starting high school, I broke my leg in PE class. It was the first time I had broken a bone, and the pain was awful.

I took time off to recover and didn't return until a month after the official school year began. I missed military training too.

Because I'd missed several months of lessons, I couldn't return to the advanced class and had to join the Olympiad class instead. I kept spending my time on games and novels, and my grades fell close to the bottom of the class. When classes were reassigned in the second year, I was moved out of the Olympiad class into the most ordinary class.

**I went from the school's top class to the Olympiad class, then to a regular class—all while gaming. A bit surreal, right?**

## I Worked Hard to Catch Up in My Second Year

I finally got serious in the second semester of my second year. Games suddenly stopped feeling fun. DNF no longer appealed to me; killing monsters and collecting gear felt pointless. What good were max-level characters if I had no money?

My mother was surprised and asked, “Why did you stop playing?” She rarely restricted my gaming because she thought self-discipline had to come from me.

I threw myself into studying and genuinely enjoyed it. Even though evening study ended around 11 p.m., I didn't feel tired; I felt happy and fulfilled.

**My effort paid off quickly: I returned to the Olympiad class.** There were about seven science-track regular classes, and we ranked separately from the advanced and Olympiad classes. I often ranked first among the regular classes, sometimes about 30 points ahead of second place. My grades were strong enough that near the end of the first semester of my final year, I asked the grade director to let me join the Olympiad class.

## Sleepless Nights Before the College Entrance Exam

> **When you fail, don't blame outside factors. In the end, the problem was mine: I wasn't strong enough.** My insomnia before the exam was my own problem too. I have no one else to blame.

My experience with the college entrance exam was difficult. Honestly, it may have been the hardest time I've been through, especially at night.

I couldn't sleep on any of the nights during the exam. Many people know how painful it is to want to sleep and be unable to.

I'd never had insomnia before. Worried that I might not sleep, I asked my mother to buy a few bottles of a tonic recommended by my teacher. I still remember the brand: Aodong.

![](https://oss.javaguide.cn/about-the-author/internet-addiction-teenager/image-20220625194714247.png)

I think my insomnia may have been related to drinking that tonic, or I may simply have been too nervous. Those nights, it felt as though ants were crawling all over me, and I developed small bumps that looked a bit like an allergic reaction.

To be clear, so no one gets the wrong idea: **not being able to sleep was my own issue. I'm not blaming the tonic.** I also checked reviews of it on several platforms and found that people generally rated it well, for reasons similar to my teacher's recommendation. If you have trouble sleeping, consult a medical professional before trying it.

I didn't perform well on the exam. I felt dazed the whole time. When the results came out, they were dozens of points below my estimate, and I ended up at a non-Project-985, non-Project-211 university. Fortunately, I chose a good major and benefited from the growth of the computer industry. I also worked hard during college.

## College Life

I had a full college life. Sometimes I'd code all night; other times, I'd go for a late-night walk along the old city wall with classmates or spend the night playing _League of Legends_ at an internet café.

I wrote about college in [“So, I've Been Out of College for Three Years!”](./my-college-life.md).

## In Closing

I was a gaming addict through most of middle school, though I cut back a little in ninth grade. I didn't really lose my obsession with games until the second semester of my second year of high school.

I became less obsessed after realizing games were just entertainment and studying mattered more at the time. I wasn't especially good at games, and I couldn't make a living from them. Leveling up and fighting monsters ultimately amounted to nothing more than binary data on a computer.

**You have to realize this for yourself. Parental supervision alone is unlikely to change things. If you don't care about studying, you won't study well.**

I strongly oppose parents interfering too much in their children's lives. I also strongly object to parents blaming video games for gaming addiction or blaming films and TV for their children's violence.

**Constantly protecting your children isn't a reliable solution; sooner or later, they will face more and more temptations on their own. Some children who are overprotected by their parents fall apart when they reach college. They have no independence and no self-control when faced with temptation.**
